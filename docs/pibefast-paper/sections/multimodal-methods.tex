\section{Multimodal BE-FAST Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Common Measurement and Quality Methods}
\label{subsec:common_methods}

Pose and face quality outputs have different meanings, so modality-specific
criteria are retained rather than collapsed into one confidence score. For
MoveNet-based modules, a timestamped observation is
$\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{pose}}_t=\{(x_{t,k},y_{t,k},c_{t,k})\}$, where $c_{t,k}$
is the detector score for body landmark $k$. A feature accepts a pose frame
only if all required landmarks satisfy
$c_{t,k}\geq c_{\min}^{\mathrm{pose}}$ and its body scale is non-degenerate.
Thus, $c_{\min}^{\mathrm{pose}}$ is an acquisition gate, not a calibrated
probability of landmark correctness.

MediaPipe instead returns face landmarks
$\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{face}}_t=\{(x_{t,k},y_{t,k},z_{t,k})\}$; $z_{t,k}$ is a
relative depth coordinate, not landmark confidence; detection, presence, and
tracking thresholds operate at model level. An eye or face frame is accepted
only when a face and all required indices are returned and task-specific
geometric, pixel-scale, pose, and temporal-stability gates pass. Invalid
coordinates are discarded, not imputed. If $N_v$ of $N$ acquired frames pass
the complete feature-specific rule, trial quality includes

\begin{equation}
q_{\mathrm{valid}}=N_v/N.
\label{eq:valid_fraction}
\end{equation}

Decisions require both $N_v\geq N_{\min}$ and
$q_{\mathrm{valid}}\geq q_{\min}$, ensuring sufficient absolute evidence and
preventing invalid frames from dominating long windows. Both thresholds are
calibrated for the deployed camera.

Image-plane body distances are divided by shoulder width $d_s$. Face
coordinates are centred between the outer eye corners, aligned to their axis,
and divided by interocular distance $d_i$; iris displacement is additionally
normalized within each palpebral fissure. These similarity normalizations
reduce translation, in-plane roll, and uniform-scale effects, but not
perspective or out-of-plane rotation. The eye module estimates within-trial
pitch, yaw, and roll ranges from the MediaPipe canonical-face transform and
rejects excessive head rotation. Eye-axis alignment reduces face-module roll,
while pitch and yaw remain potential sources of variation.

All decisions use temporal windows. For a valid scalar sequence $X=\{x_t\}$,
the median and median absolute deviation (MAD) are

\begin{equation}
\widetilde{x}=\operatorname{median}(X),\quad
\operatorname{MAD}(X)=\operatorname{median}|x_t-\widetilde{x}|,
\label{eq:robust_statistics}
\end{equation}

with $\operatorname{IQR}(X)=Q_{0.75}(X)-Q_{0.25}(X)$
\cite{iglewicz1993outliers}. Ratio metrics use $\epsilon=10^{-9}$ solely for
numerical stability. Quantiles follow an empirical nearest-index rule for
reproducible short sequences. Medians and percentile ranges limit the influence
of anomalous trials and extreme frames, respectively.

All components use the same four-state semantics:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{positive}: a new symptom is reported or a quality-qualified
    measurement meets a prespecified warning rule;
    \item \textit{negative}: a completed, quality-qualified task does not
    satisfy the corresponding warning rule;
    \item \textit{insufficient}: acquisition, visibility, action, or signal
    quality prevents interpretation; and
    \item \textit{skipped}: the user intentionally did not complete the task.
\end{itemize}

Only a valid negative counts as a completed negative measurement. Insufficient
and skipped results remain missing during fusion and are never treated as
normal. Positive results are screening warnings, not stroke diagnoses, and a
negative camera result cannot exclude stroke.

\subsection{Balance (B)}
\label{subsec:balance}

The balance module first asks about sudden imbalance, incoordination, or gait
difficulty. A new symptom takes precedence, and an unsafe participant does not
stand. Otherwise, with a caregiver nearby, the camera records a 30-s quiet-
standing window. The first five qualified windows form a fixed personal
baseline for subsequent comparisons.

\paragraph{Protocol and measurements}
The camera remains fixed in an approximately frontal view. MoveNet provides
bilateral shoulder, hip, and ankle landmarks. Let $\mathbf{s}_t$,
$\mathbf{h}_t$, and $\mathbf{a}_t$ denote the respective bilateral midpoints,
and let $d_{s,t}$ be shoulder width. With image $x$ increasing rightward and
$y$ downward, we define the image-plane trunk inclination $\theta_t$ in radians and
the normalized horizontal position $u_t$ of the trunk centre relative to the
ankle midpoint as

\begin{align}
\theta_t=\operatorname{atan2}
(s_{t,x}-h_{t,x},\ h_{t,y}-s_{t,y}),\\
u_t=\frac{(s_{t,x}+h_{t,x})/2-a_{t,x}}{d_{s,t}}.
\label{eq:trunk_angle}
\end{align}

Here $\operatorname{atan2}$ returns the angle directly in radians,
$(s_{t,x}+h_{t,x})/2$ averages the shoulder and hip midpoints horizontally,
and shoulder-width division makes displacement dimensionless. The frontal view
lets image horizontal approximate the anatomical mediolateral axis;
$\theta_t$ remains camera-relative, so repositioning requires
recalibration. A valid window contains all six landmarks in at least $N_{\min}$
standing frames and satisfies $q_{\mathrm{valid}}\geq q_{\min}$. We summarize it by

\begin{align}
\widetilde{\theta}&=\operatorname{median}(\theta_t),\\
R_{90}&=Q_{0.95}(u)-Q_{0.05}(u),\\
v_{\mathrm{ML}}&=
\frac{\sum_{t=2}^{N_v}|u_t-u_{t-1}|}{t_{N_v}-t_1},
\label{eq:balance_features}
\end{align}

$\widetilde{\theta}$ captures sustained inclination,
$v_{\mathrm{ML}}$ captures time-normalized lateral motion, and $R_{90}$ captures
the central 90\% sway range while suppressing extreme frames.

\paragraph{Personal-baseline decision}
For $j\in\mathcal{J}_{B}=
\{\widetilde{\theta},v_{\mathrm{ML}},R_{90}\}$, the five calibration
windows define the robust centre and scale

\begin{align}
\mu_j&=\operatorname{median}(X_j^{\mathrm{base}}),\\
\sigma_j&=\max\left\{1.4826\operatorname{MAD}(X_j^{\mathrm{base}}),
\frac{\operatorname{IQR}(X_j^{\mathrm{base}})}{1.349}\right\}.
\label{eq:robust_scale}
\end{align}

Five windows form a short odd-length baseline, giving a unique median centre
and allowing dispersion to be estimated while balancing calibration information
against five 30-s tasks. The factors 1.4826 and 1.349 put MAD and IQR on a
normal-consistent standard-deviation scale; taking the larger estimate limits
score inflation from a short baseline.

A later window yields

\begin{align}
r_{\theta}&=\frac{|\widetilde{\theta}_{\mathrm{current}}
-\mu_{\theta}|}{\sigma_{\theta}},\\
r_v&=\frac{\max(0,v_{\mathrm{ML,current}}-\mu_v)}{\sigma_v},\\
r_R&=\frac{\max(0,R_{90,\mathrm{current}}-\mu_R)}{\sigma_R}.
\label{eq:balance_change_scores}
\end{align}

Inclination is tested in both directions, whereas sway is tested only for
increases. We set $\tau=3.5$ from the Iglewicz--Hoaglin modified-$Z$ outlier
rule \cite{iglewicz1993outliers}; with the conservative scale above, it selects
marked departures from personal baseline:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_{B}^{\mathrm{camera}}=
[r_{\theta}\geq\tau]\ \lor\
\bigl([r_v\geq\tau]\land[r_R\geq\tau]\bigr).
\label{eq:balance_decision}
\end{equation}

Requiring both sway velocity and range reduces alerts from local landmark
jitter. Failed quality gates or undefined nonzero scores yield
\textit{insufficient}; the first five qualified windows are
\textit{calibrating}; later windows are positive when
$\mathcal{D}_{B}^{\mathrm{camera}}$ holds and negative otherwise. The baseline
remains frozen, while its length, threshold, and conjunction are calibrated in
the target population.

\paragraph{Interpretation and scope}
The 30-s window and velocity feature follow quiet-standing studies
\cite{aryan2023forceplate,ruhe2010cop}. They support contactless longitudinal
measurement, provided camera placement, sampling, and stance remain consistent.
Two-dimensional pose is not a complete balance examination, and a negative
result does not exclude impairment or stroke
\cite{yeung2014kinect,dai2021lateropulsion}; test--retest reliability is an
explicit validation endpoint.

\subsection{Eyes (E)}
\label{subsec:eyes}

We use the pretrained MediaPipe Face Landmarker to extract iris and eye-corner
landmarks \cite{bazarevsky2020mediapipeiris,google2022facemeshmodelcard}.
Before roll correction, separately normalized $x/y$ coordinates are mapped to
an isotropic, frame-width-based plane; outer-canthus distance provides geometric
scale.

Sudden visual loss, field loss, diplopia, or sustained gaze deviation takes
priority over camera findings. Otherwise, a rest--centre--lateral sequence gives
three responses per side in reversed order. Three supports a median while
tolerating one atypical trial; the first response is retained for the repetition
ablation. Each lateral endpoint is paired with its preceding centre to reduce
drift. Target timing, viewing geometry, achieved angle $A_{\mathrm{ach}}$, and
the fixed camera-mirroring convention are recorded with the trial.

For eye $e$, horizontal iris position within the palpebral fissure is

\begin{equation}
g_{e,t}=\frac{x_{e,t}^{\mathrm{iris}}-x_{e,t}^{\min}}
{x_{e,t}^{\max}-x_{e,t}^{\min}}.
\label{eq:gaze_normalization}
\end{equation}

Here $g$ denotes normalized horizontal iris position. Let
$j\in\{1,2,3\}$ index repetitions, $d\in\{-1,+1\}$ denote target direction,
and $s_c\in\{-1,+1\}$ be the prespecified camera-coordinate convention. The
paired signed response is

\begin{equation}
r_{e,d,j}=s_c d\left(\widetilde{g}_{e,d,j}-
\widetilde{g}_{e,c(d,j)}\right),
\label{eq:eye_response}
\end{equation}

where $c(d,j)$ is the preceding centre and a correct response is positive.
Endpoints use the median; sample count, eye width, and MAD remain continuous
quality variables. Amplitude is
$a_{e,d}=\max\{0,\operatorname{median}_j r_{e,d,j}\}$ and binocular response is
$b_d=(a_{L,d}+a_{R,d})/2$. Directional asymmetry and direction-specific
binocular endpoint discordance are

\begin{align}
A_{\mathrm{dir}}&=\frac{|b_{-1}-b_{+1}|}
{\max(b_{-1},b_{+1},\epsilon)},\\
C_d&=\frac{|a_{L,d}-a_{R,d}|}
{\max(a_{L,d},a_{R,d},\epsilon)}.
\label{eq:eye_asymmetry_conjugacy}
\end{align}

$C_d$ compares the eyes within each target direction. Resting bias is expressed
as a task-calibrated angular proxy:

\begin{equation}
\theta^{\mathrm{proxy}}_{e}=s_c
\frac{g_{e,\mathrm{rest}}-\operatorname{median}(g_{e,\mathrm{centre}})}
{|g_{e,+1}-g_{e,-1}|}\,2A_{\mathrm{ach}}.
\label{eq:eye_rest_proxy}
\end{equation}

The factor two is the span from $-A_{\mathrm{ach}}$ to $+A_{\mathrm{ach}}$.
Assuming accurate fixation and locally linear iris response,
$\theta^{\mathrm{proxy}}_e$ is task-specific; it, $A_{\mathrm{dir}}$, and $C_d$
are validated as continuous phenotypes, separately from imaging gaze angles.

Quality records include sample count, valid-frame fraction, eye width, endpoint
MAD, head-pose coverage and range, and between-repeat dispersion. Missing
landmarks or an uncomputable endpoint yields \textit{insufficient}; otherwise
the continuous metrics enter the healthy-participant failure and repeatability
analyses. Sudden visual symptoms independently raise the E warning.

Stroke can involve conjugate deviation and directional ocular hypokinesia
\cite{singer2006gaze,kudo2021eyemovement}. Because sampling rate affects saccade
detection even at 60 versus 120~Hz \cite{leube2017sampling}, this low-rate task
reports stable endpoints; latency, velocity, pursuit gain, and nystagmus require
comparison with clinical video-oculography.

\subsection{Face (F)}
\label{subsec:face}

We similarly use the pretrained MediaPipe Face Landmarker to extract facial
landmarks and expression blendshape scores
\cite{google2022facemeshmodelcard}. The face task compares a prompted smile with
the participant's neutral expression. After eye-axis alignment and interocular
normalization, mouth-corner height is

\begin{equation}
m_t=\frac{y_{t,\mathrm{left\ corner}}-
y_{t,\mathrm{right\ corner}}}{d_{i,t}}.
\label{eq:mouth_corner_difference}
\end{equation}

and its neutral-referenced change is

\begin{equation}
\Delta m=\operatorname{median}(m_t^{\mathrm{smile}})-
\operatorname{median}(m_t^{\mathrm{neutral}}).
\label{eq:mouth_corner_delta}
\end{equation}

MediaPipe smile blendshapes add $\Delta a=a_R-a_L$ and action strength
$a_{\max}=\max(a_L,a_R)$, where $a_L,a_R\in[0,1]$ are the left and right
mouth-smile activation scores \cite{google2022facemeshmodelcard}. The current
implementation uses $a_{\max}\geq0.22$ to confirm a measurable smile, followed
by engineering warning references of $|\Delta m|\geq0.075$ interocular units or
$|\Delta a|\geq0.24$. The value 0.22 gates action completion; 0.075 and 0.24 act
on the geometric and model-score paths, respectively, giving both paths explicit,
reproducible boundaries.

Insufficient frames, small face scale, or $a_{\max}<0.22$ yields
\textit{insufficient}, preventing an absent smile from becoming asymmetry. In an
approximately frontal view, either engineering reference crossing raises a
warning; the sign gives the visibly weaker side. All three values are normalized
implementation parameters; target-population experiments use continuous
$\Delta m$, $\Delta a$, and $a_{\max}$ to estimate and independently validate
operating points. Neutral referencing reduces, but does not remove, confounding
from stable anatomical or habitual asymmetry.

\subsection{Arms (A)}
\label{subsec:arms}

The BE-FAST observation of bilateral arm raising and unilateral drift provides
the clinical-action basis for the arm task \cite{aroor2017befast}. MoveNet capture
follows a fixed sequence: both arms start lowered, rise to approximately
shoulder-height lateral extension, hold for 5~s, and return. Five seconds defines
the technical hold window, providing a stable sequence and first-versus-last-third
drift comparison while limiting task burden; an incomplete sequence is
\textit{insufficient}.

Let $d_{s,t}$ denote the Euclidean distance between the two shoulder landmarks.
For side $e\in\{L,R\}$, normalized wrist height and lateral reach are

\begin{align}
h_{e,t}&=\frac{y_{e,t}^{\mathrm{wrist}}-
y_{e,t}^{\mathrm{shoulder}}}{d_{s,t}},
\label{eq:arm_height}\\
r_{e,t}&=\frac{|x_{e,t}^{\mathrm{wrist}}-
x_{e,t}^{\mathrm{shoulder}}|}{d_{s,t}}.
\label{eq:arm_reach}
\end{align}

Because image $y$ increases downward, larger $h_{e,t}$ means a lower wrist.
The start gate is $h_{L,t},h_{R,t}\geq0.45$; within 8~s, the raise gate requires
$h_{L,t},h_{R,t}\leq0.42$, elbow angles $\geq125^{\circ}$, and
$r_{L,t},r_{R,t}\geq0.45$ for 0.45~s. Hold analysis requires 20 valid frames,
a valid fraction $\geq0.55$, and continued gate compliance; both wrists then
return to the lowered start position within 5~s. These values define action and
quality gates: the initial 0.45 confirms that both wrists are below the shoulders;
0.42, $125^{\circ}$, and 0.45 constrain wrist height, elbow extension, and lateral
reach; 0.45~s rejects transient crossings; 20 frames and 0.55 provide absolute
sample support and relative coverage; and 8~s and 5~s bound interaction timeouts.

For accepted hold frames, the instantaneous bilateral level difference is
$D_t=h_{L,t}-h_{R,t}$ and the persistent level feature is
$\widetilde D=\operatorname{median}_t(D_t)$. Drift compares the first and last
thirds of the hold:

\begin{align}
\delta_e&=\operatorname{median}(h_{e}^{\mathrm{late}})-
\operatorname{median}(h_{e}^{\mathrm{early}}),\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{drift}}&=\delta_L-\delta_R.
\label{eq:arm_drift}
\end{align}

A qualified trial is positive when $|\widetilde D|\geq0.30$ or
$|\Delta_{\mathrm{drift}}|\geq0.22$. These prespecified engineering references,
expressed in shoulder-width-normalized units, apply to persistent level and
within-hold drift and provide reproducible software-boundary inputs. If both
cross, the larger reference-normalized magnitude determines laterality; positive
denotes a lower or more-dropping left wrist. Continuous and binary results are
both retained so target-population data can re-estimate and independently
validate the operating points.

\subsection{Speech}
\label{subsec:speech}

The speech module adopts a two-level strategy combining passive natural-speech
monitoring with triggered active assessment. Passive monitoring evaluates
temporal, phonatory, articulatory, and resonance-related changes relative to an
individual's baseline, while active assessment uses a fixed sentence to obtain
more structured evidence after an abnormality signal.

\begin{center}
  \includegraphics[width=0.84\columnwidth]{\paperroot figures/speech-architecture-original.png}
\end{center}

For passive natural speech, valid speech segments are represented using
lightweight acoustic descriptors. The current implementation uses a
90-dimensional representation consisting of 40-bin Log-Mel means and standard
deviations together with ten temporal, energy, and spectral descriptors. A
standardized logistic regression model maps the representation to a
dysarthria-related probability:

\begin{equation}
p=\sigma\left(\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{z}+b\right),\quad
z_i=\frac{x_i-\mu_i}{s_i}.
\end{equation}

Where $\mu_i$ and $s_i$ are estimated from the training data. The model is
trained on control and dysarthric Mandarin speech from the MDSC dataset and is
used directly in the Speech screening decision. The exported model threshold is
$\tau_{\mathrm{M}}=0.807859$. For quality-qualified passive speech,
$p\geq\tau_{\mathrm{M}}$ immediately triggers the active assessment and the
window is excluded from the personal baseline. To account for inter-speaker
variability, the remaining acoustic changes are interpreted relative to the
individual's baseline.

For active fixed-sentence assessment, triggered speech first passes an
audio-quality gate to exclude recordings with insufficient duration, excessive
low-level signal, or clipping. The valid segment is then transcribed offline
using a locally deployed Whisper-based system. When a reference sentence is
available, transcription discrepancy is quantified by Character Error Rate
(CER):

\begin{equation}
CER=\frac{S+D+I}{N}.
\end{equation}

where $(S)$, $(D)$, and $(I)$ denote substitution, deletion, and insertion
errors, and $(N)$ is the number of reference characters. Speech delivery is
further characterized by pause fraction and speech rate:

\begin{equation}
PF=\frac{T_{pause}}{T_{total}},\quad
CPS=\frac{N_{char}}{T_{speech}}.
\end{equation}

The MDSC model is also applied to the quality-qualified guided recording. In the
current implementation, the Speech result is abnormal when
$p\geq\tau_{\mathrm{M}}$, CER $\geq 0.35$, pause fraction $\geq 0.55$, or CPS is
outside 1--8. These thresholds are system-level screening rules and are not
independent clinical diagnostic criteria. Overall, the two-level design
combines MDSC-based dysarthria detection, individualized continuous monitoring,
and structured speech assessment in the formal Speech decision.

\subsection{Digital Medical Record Analysis}
\label{subsec:medical_record}
