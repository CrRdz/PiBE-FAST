\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experimental results indicate that PiBE-FAST can deploy a full BE-FAST
screening workflow on edge hardware with interpretability in all intermediate
outputs across multiple sensing modalities. Instead of relying on one visual
observation, the proposed approach leverages the balance, eye movement, facial
symmetry, arm movement, speech analysis, and structured clinical information
through a quality-gated multimodal fusion framework. Such design allows each
component to retain their own interpretability, but to preserve the information
across modalities.

One of the key engineering innovations is the efficiency of the stage-aware
inference scheduling technique. Instead of activating both MoveNet and
MediaPipe at all time, the system activates each model only during the
corresponding stage of the BE-FAST screening process. Such inference scheduling
technique helps to reduce unnecessary CPU load and thermal stress of Raspberry
Pi and ensures responsive interaction during guided screening. In comparison
with simultaneous execution of multiple vision models, the stage-aware
scheduling may become a more practical deployment technique for a low-power
device.

Quality gating mechanisms enhance deployment robustness of the system in real
world settings. Instead of making each observation based on binary criteria,
each modality is evaluated based on confidence filtering, landmark visibility,
valid-frame ratio and statistical consistency across multiple frames before
being included in the multimodal feature vector. Such strategy prevents
low-quality measurements caused by occlusion, unstable posture, lighting
changes, and low-quality audio data from being recognized as a valid clinical
observation, making the system suitable for home-screening setting.

Speech subsystem contributes to visual screening with both linguistic and
acoustic information. Offline Whisper transcription enables fixed-sentence
speech recognition without cloud-based services, and CER quantifies the
disagreement between the recognized and prompted sentences. The MDSC-trained
acoustic model separately estimates a dysarthria-related probability. For
passive monitoring, fundamental frequency, jitter, shimmer,
harmonics-to-noise ratio, pause properties, and speaking rate are compared with
the individual baseline. The fixed fusion vector retains four Speech features:
CER, characters per second, pause fraction, and MDSC dysarthria probability.

Even though the multimodal architecture has many engineering benefits in
contrast with isolated facial and body-pose analysis, the current realization
of the system should be considered as a research prototype, not a clinically
validated diagnostic tool. The safety-rule framework, therefore, is separate
from the research fusion layer, making sure that engineering experiments do not
override the conservative emergency screening procedure.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

Several limitations should be considered before proceeding to clinical
evaluation.

First of all, the current experiment mainly evaluates the engineering
performance of the system rather than its diagnostic capabilities. The
thresholds used in the system (quality gates, modified Z-score detection, and
speech feature boundaries) are engineering parameters and not clinically
validated diagnostic cutoffs. As a result, negative screening does not rule out
the possibility of stroke.

Secondly, speech experiments rely on the Mandarin Dysarthric Speech Corpus,
which contains chronic dysarthric speech rather than acute-stroke cases. The
MDSC model therefore contributes directly to the Speech screening status using
its frozen engineering threshold, but its corpus labels do not establish
acute-stroke sensitivity, specificity, or calibration.

Thirdly, the visual subsystem uses the monocular RGB camera. Body sway, eye
movement, and arm position estimations are kinematic proxies and not direct
physiological measurements obtained with force platform or eye tracking
equipment or neurological examination. Moreover, simulating abnormal movements
by healthy participants does not perfectly reproduce the true symptoms of
stroke.

Future work will involve gathering of the clinically annotated acute stroke
datasets, adding stroke mimic control groups, evaluation of the stability of
the Raspberry Pi deployment, improvement of multilingual speech processing,
and investigation of the learnable multimodal fusion models while preserving
the safety-rule framework.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we have presented PiBE-FAST, a quality-gated multimodal BE-FAST
screening framework for privacy-preserving edge deployment on Raspberry Pi. The
proposed system leverages the balance, eye movement, facial symmetry, arm
movement, speech analysis, and structured clinical information through
stage-aware inference scheduling and local multimodal fusion while retaining
the interpretability of intermediate outputs for each screening component.

The engineering evaluation demonstrated that multimodal BE-FAST screening is
possible on resource-constrained hardware with local processing, robust quality
control, and offline speech analysis. However, the proposed framework remains a
research prototype and not a clinically validated diagnostic tool. Further work
requires large scale clinical validation, standardization of acute stroke
cohorts, stroke-mimic controls, and prospective evaluation.
