\section{Related Work}
Digital screening systems have combined facial video and speech \cite{cai2022deepstroke}, neurological examination landmarks \cite{lee2022skeleton}, and multimodal movement evidence \cite{ou2025multimodal}. These studies motivate combining observable signs in a screening interface. PiBE-FAST studies their use in a monitoring-to-check workflow, with explicit evaluation of scripted-sign response and reference-negative triggering.

Camera landmarks support contactless measurements of body sway and ocular position \cite{yeung2014kinect,bazarevsky2020mediapipeiris}. Their interpretation depends on visibility, pose, action completion, and camera conditions. Quiet-standing measures motivate balance assessment \cite{ruhe2010cop}, whereas clinical ocular assessment requires references beyond uncalibrated RGB geometry \cite{kudo2021eyemovement}. Mandarin corpora support dysarthria modeling \cite{gao2024mdsc}, and Whisper provides a separate transcription output \cite{radford2023whisper}. PiBE-FAST assigns these measurements distinct roles in acquisition, decision-making, and inspection.

Event sourcing records state changes as events and supports reconstruction of earlier states \cite{fowler2005eventsourcing}; provenance models describe the entities, activities, and agents behind data \cite{groth2013prov}. PiBE-FAST applies this separation of current state and history to BE-FAST-specific contracts: acquisition failure cannot negate a symptom, ordinary retries cannot retract it, and corrections must preserve earlier reports. The domain specification determines which events change active evidence: a negative retry retains an independent symptom, an explicit correction retracts it, and an urgent report can remain incomplete. These interaction rules support interpretable warning reports when measurements and symptom histories change.
