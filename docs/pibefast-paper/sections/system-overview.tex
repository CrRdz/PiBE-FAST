\section{System Overview}
\label{sec:system_overview}

PiBE-FAST uses a two-tier local architecture because long-running observation
and fine-grained guided measurement have different sensing requirements. This
split also avoids forcing every model and camera viewpoint onto one low-power
device.

\subsection{Always-on Raspberry Pi Tier}

Balance (B) and Speech (S) form the low-load passive tier on the Raspberry Pi.
For B, the Pi consumes a continuously reachable IP-camera stream and applies
throttled MoveNet inference for 24/7 observation of visible, pose-qualified
standing. Periods with no person, incomplete body visibility, or an ineligible
stance are unmeasurable rather than negative. For S, a USB, I$^2$S, or ALSA
microphone attached directly to the Pi supplies rolling natural-speech windows.
The passive speech process compares local features with a personal baseline; it
does not run Whisper continuously, and routine raw WAV files are deleted after
feature extraction. Thus, the IP camera supplies B video only, whereas S audio
comes from the Pi-local microphone. In both modalities, sustained change requests
follow-up and is not itself a stroke diagnosis.

\subsection{Guided Local-Computer Tier}

Eyes (E), Face (F), and Arms (A) are short active screens performed with the
built-in or USB webcam of a local computer. Distant, low-rate Pi video provides
insufficient iris and face pixel scale and is more vulnerable to occlusion,
perspective, and wrist/elbow landmark error. The interface therefore guides the
participant through close, stable, and explicitly phased tasks: centre/lateral
gaze for E, neutral-to-smile motion for F, and a complete bilateral
raise--hold--lower action for A. The corresponding MediaPipe or MoveNet inference
runs locally on the computer and is activated only during the selected task.

The Raspberry Pi retains the Web service, local history, and passive monitoring
state. The local computer provides guidance and returns structured active-task
measurements over the local network; no raw stream requires a third-party cloud.
The repository also retains a single-host mode for development and demonstration,
but the split topology above is the target deployment evaluated in this paper.
